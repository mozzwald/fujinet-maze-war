# 04-06 — Let the remote follow catch up

> **Status 2026-09-09 (final): retracted. Do not execute.**
> Two separate rig bugs (documented in `tests/rig/README.md` and at the top of
> `04-RESEARCH.md`) produced every measurement this plan was built on, including
> the `MOVEST != 0` / `RF_SNAP` suspect that superseded the original premise
> below. With both bugs fixed, `converge.py` was run against unmodified HEAD
> across 0-50% loss at 120ms delay, many runs: **remote reconciliation converges
> to zero gap in 100% of samples once the target is genuinely still, at every
> loss rate tested, and never reaches the 3-cell/10-cell recovery/snap
> thresholds while moving even at 50% loss.** There is no stuck-arrears bug for
> a catch-up mechanism to fix. Any further improvement to the residual
> while-moving lag is a render-state-separation problem (`04-02`/`04-03`), not a
> `REMOTE_FOLLOW` bug fix. This plan is kept for the record, not for execution.

## Why

Measured, not assumed: under 120 ms one-way delay with 3 % loss, a remote actor
is **one cell behind the authoritative cell in 100 % of samples**, in a single
unbroken run. With delay alone it is behind 47 % of the time, including a 5.6 %
tail at eight cells. On a clean loopback it is never behind. The full table and
the rig are in `04-RESEARCH.md`.

The cause is structural and small. `REMOTE_FOLLOW` advances a remote actor at
most one cell per move tick, and only when the next cell in the actor's
authoritative joy direction equals the authoritative cell. It walks at exactly
the rate the server does. So the gap is never opened *and never closed*: one
dropped snapshot puts the actor one cell in arrears for as long as it keeps
moving, and nothing but a divergence threshold ever resets it.

One cell is a whole animation period — roughly 100 ms of visual lag stacked on
top of the real link delay, and it is the bulk of what makes watching the other
player's screen hard. Removing it does not need render state, so it should not
wait for `04-02`.

## Shape

Give the follow a backlog notion and let it spend more than one step per move
tick while the backlog is non-empty, with a hard cap.

- Keep the existing single-step path exactly as it is when the actor is one cell
  or less from its target. Nothing about the common case should change.
- When the manhattan gap is two or more, allow a second step in the same move
  tick. Two is the cap: it halves a backlog quickly without becoming a visible
  jump, and it stays well under `NET_RECOVER_P1` (3), so the existing recovery
  and hard-snap guards keep their meaning untouched.
- The step must remain a *walk*: same direction derivation, same
  `NET_AHEAD_FREE` obstacle test, same animation. Catch-up is permission to take
  another ordinary step, never permission to skip one.
- Never overshoot. If the extra step would land on or past the authoritative
  cell, do not take it — the actor stops where the server says it is.

## Risks

- **Bricks.** A second step must run the same look-ahead as the first, or the
  catch-up walks a remote actor through a wall. The existing `NET_AHEAD_FREE`
  call has to be inside whatever loop the second step goes through, not hoisted.
- **Sound and animation.** `MOVEIM` drives the walk sound and the shuffle
  animation phase. Two steps in one tick must not double-trigger either; check
  `MOVSND` behaviour before and after.
- **Making the snap worse.** Faster convergence could mask the 8-cell case
  rather than fix it, and that case is `04-03`'s job. Report both rows of the
  table, not just the one-cell row, so a shifted problem is visible as a shifted
  problem.

## Acceptance

Run the `04-RESEARCH` rig at 120 ms / 3 % and 120 ms / 0 %, same durations, and
publish the same table. The one-cell share must fall substantially at 3 % loss;
the eight-cell share must not grow at 0 % loss. Then a human session on real
hardware, playing from one machine while watching the other screen, which is the
test that actually decides whether it feels better.
